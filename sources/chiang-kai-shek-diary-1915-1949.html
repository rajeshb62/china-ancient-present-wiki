Chiang Kai-shek’s Diary: Selected Entries 1915–1949 (Jiang Jieshi riji 蒋介石日记) — Chiang Kai-shek 蒋介石, compiled by 偶然童子

What this is

蒋介石日记（1915-1949） (Jiang Jieshi riji, “Chiang Kai-shek’s Diary 1915–1949”) is a curated compilation of dated diary entries attributed to Chiang Kai-shek (蔣介石 / 蒋中正; here addressed throughout as 余, “I”). The text is framed as authored by 蒋介石 and compiled / prepared by ☆偶然童子☆ — an unofficial internet compiler’s e-text, not an archival edition. It selects 185 dated entries spread across nineteen years of a thirty-four-year span, pairing each date with Chiang’s recorded reflections on the events around it.

This is not the full, continuous diary. The genuine Chiang diary manuscripts are preserved at the Hoover Institution (Stanford) and were long kept sealed; this file is a selective popular compilation that jumps between years and annotates the text. It should be read as a widely circulated selection of diary passages, not a complete scholarly transcript. Its value for this wiki is that it preserves, in Chiang’s own first-person voice, his wartime and civil-war framing of events — the Japanese invasion and surrender, the Chinese Communist Party (共匪 / 共党 in his usage), the Marshall Mission, and the 1949 collapse.

How it is organized

The text proceeds chronologically by year, with several years subdivided (e.g. 1916年 1 / 1916年 2; 1937年 1–8; 1938年 1–4; 1942年 1–2; 1943年 3; 1945年 5–8; 1946年 4; 1949年 2). Under each year run dated entries in 【月 日】 brackets. Two entries are extended set-pieces: the 西安半月记 fragment (December 12, 1936, the Xi’an Incident) and the 1938 元旦 essay on the war.

Chapter breakdown (year by year)

Style: English title + (pinyin + 中文) + multi-line gloss of what Chiang’s selected entries record. All views are Chiang’s own first-person framing, as preserved by the compiler.

Early revolutionary years (1915–1919)

1915 年. Brief, event-anchored notes: Yuan Shikai accepting Japan’s Twenty-One Demands (5月9日); 杨度、孙毓筠、严复 et al. launching the 筹安会 to promote monarchy (8月14日); the party (总理 = Sun Yat-sen) dispatching fundraisers abroad for the anti-Yuan campaign (9月30日); Cai E (蔡锷) organizing the 护国军 uprising in Yunnan (12月25日).

1916 年 1–2. Chiang’s service in the anti-Yuan revolutionary northeast army. The assassination of Chen Qimei (陳其美) by Yuan’s agents in a Japanese resident’s home (5月18日) — “余哭之哀，收其尸以归…自撰文祭之.” Then long operational passages from his time with the 中华革命党东北军 headquarters at 潍县 (Weixian): daily orders, inspection tours, telegraph traffic, handling discipline complaints against his troops, coordinating with Japanese guard detachments — vivid records of an officer’s routine in the anti-Yuan campaign.

1917–1919 年. Scattered entries: the early revolutionary period and events around the party’s rebuilding. (Compiled entries thin for these years.)

The Central Plains War and prewar crisis (1930–1936)

1930 年. Reflections amid the Central Plains War (中原大战): the flux of alliances among warlords (“联此制彼或联彼制此，皆非正本之道”), the need to strengthen one’s own forces and let rivals exhaust each other, and wartime resolve (“唯有坚忍镇静，维系军心…若至万不得已，唯有一死以报党国”). Later entries note 东北 forces joining the central front in effect if not in open combat.

1931–1935 年. Sparse selection: the Mukden Incident era (1931 九一八) and the subsequent years of Japanese pressure and internal consolidation. (Compiled entries are light.)

1936 年. The run-up to the Xi’an Incident. Chiang despairs over the national humiliation and counsels forbearance toward Japan (“三年之内，倭寇不能灭中国…但此时当不可不隐忍”), and increasingly distrusts Zhang Xueliang (汉卿 / 张学良) over his reluctance to campaign against the communists. The centerpiece is the 西安半月记 fragment (12月12日): Chiang’s own account of the night of the mutiny — gunfire at the Huaging Pool residence, his escape over the wall and up the mountain, and the dawning realization of a planned coup rather than a local mutiny. Followed by relief that the central authorities were managing the crisis (12月16日).

The War of Resistance (1937–1938)

1937 年 (8 month-sections). From the Marco Polo Bridge (七七, 7月8日 entry) through the fall campaigns. The entries chart Chiang’s decision to resist, the shift of the war to Shanghai and the interior, and his conviction that the conflict must be long and that China’s perseverance would ultimately turn the international situation.

1938 年 (4 month-sections). Chiang’s wartime diary at length:

  • 元旦 essay on foreign policy: neither Japan (倭祸急) nor the Soviet danger (俄患隐) should be relied upon; China must stand self-sufficient and independent.
  • Refusal to capitulate — “与其屈服而亡，不如战败而亡” — and dismissal of Japanese threats to deny recognition to the National Government.
  • The propaganda value of the Taierzhuang victory (台儿庄胜利, 4月30日) for morale.
  • Dismay at internal wavering (e.g. 高宗武’s freelancing) and at the maneuvers among 近卫, 坂垣, 宇垣 over whether to seek a negotiated settlement with China.
  • Faith that Japan’s failure to win quickly was fatal, and that a second world war loomed in which China must prepare its role.

Wartime diplomacy and the end of the war (1940–1945)

1940 年 5. Note of US $100 million loan to China (12月7日), half in currency-stabilization support — Chiang credits Roosevelt’s genuine assistance and marks it as the most substantial international help since the war began.

1942–1943 年. The war in the Pacific context. (Compiled entries selective.)

1945 年 5–8. The end of the war:

  • Hong Kong surrender dispute (8月26–28日, 8月31日, 9月1日): Chiang insists, as China Theater supreme commander, on appointing a British officer to receive the Japanese surrender in Hong Kong; he resists Britain’s refusal, and records satisfaction that Britain finally accepted the appointment.
  • The Chongqing talks with Mao Zedong (毛泽东 / 共毛): Chiang’s policy of sincere treatment toward Mao (8月28日), his insistence on the unity of government and military command as the non-negotiable center (8月29日), and his running record of the negotiations — frustration over Communist troop-strength demands (the 48 vs 28 divisions dispute, 9月12日, 9月17日), distrust of Communist intentions in the occupied territories (9月24日), and the farewell meetings with Mao before his return to Yan’an (10月9–11日).
  • The surrender ceremony at Nanjing on the anniversary of the Guangzhou Uprising (9月9日), set against Chiang’s worries about Soviet occupation of Manchuria, unrest in Xinjiang, and the unresolved Outer Mongolia question.
  • The atomic bomb and Soviet entry into the war (8月31日): Chiang praises science’s power to end the world’s wars, but condemns Soviet opportunism in declaring war on Japan.

The Marshall Mission and the civil war (1946)

1946 年 4. The collapse of the Marshall Mission mediation. Chiang records that Marshall, disturbed by the CPC’s “July 7 declaration,” still leaned toward accommodating the communists while pressuring the Nationalists (7月13日). (Compiled entries selective for this year.)

The collapse and 1949 (1949 年 2, tail)

The compilation closes in 1949 with the Nationalist collapse and Chiang’s retirement/retreat:

  • Entries on the Communist rejection of the Li Zongren government and the failure of the peace talks (2月5日, 3月28日, 4月17日, 4月30日): Chiang scorns the “surrenderist” faction and the peace negotiators, and interprets the breakdown of talks as the moment the Republic’s vitality and the renewal of the national revolution must be sought.
  • The China White Paper (8月10日): Chiang denounces Marshall and Acheson for seeking to cover their China-policy failure by destroying US–China friendship, calling the 1949 White Paper a stain on American history.
  • Li Zongren’s flight (11月21日): Chiang condemns Li’s refusal to resign or return, judging his conduct selfish and contemptible.
  • Year-end resolution (12月25日): “从前种种，譬如昨日死，今日种种，犹如今日生” — the party, politics, economy, military, diplomacy, and education have all failed; he resolves to transform the party, reorganize the army, and begin anew.

The text ends with 【完】.

Relation to the other sources

This is the first diary / personal-account source on the shelf, and the first centered on the Nationalist (KMT) leader himself rather than on Sun Yat-sen’s doctrine or the Qing/PRC institutional histories:

SourceAngle
三民主義, 建国方略Sun Yat-sen’s program (1924)
孫逸仙傳記Sun’s life as told in a translated biography
蒋介石日记 (this file)The post-Sun KMT leader’s own wartime and civil-war reflections (1915–1949)
建国大纲理论基础 (1983)PRC-era re-appropriation of the 建国 vocabulary under Deng

Read together, the Sun sources give the republican program and the party’s founding story; this diary shows the second-generation KMT leader who inherited that program and led China through the war with Japan and into the civil war and retreat to Taiwan.

History of the Chinese Communist Party, Volume I, Lower Book (Zhongguo Gongchandang lishi 中国共产党历史 第一卷 下册), 1921–1949 — Central Party History Research Office 中共中央党史研究室

What this is

《中国共产党历史》第一卷（1921—1949）下册 is the lower half of the official two-book first volume of the History of the Chinese Communist Party, compiled by the Central Party History Research Office of the CCP (中共中央党史研究室) and issued by the CCP History Press (中共党史出版社; this file is the 2011 printing associated with DuXiu ssid 13029078). Volume I as a whole covers the party from its founding through the founding of the PRC; this lower book alone carries the narrative from the full outbreak of the War of Resistance against Japan in July 1937 through the close of the nationwide Liberation War and the eve of the new state in September 1949.

It is not a private scholarly monograph. It is the central party-history office’s authorized continuous narrative for the late revolutionary period: war, base-area construction, united-front politics, land reform, decisive campaigns, and the transfer from war to state-founding. The voice is institutional and teleological — two destinies, two futures; resistance, solidarity, and progress as the wartime line; the new-democratic revolution’s victory as the book’s terminal meaning. Readers who want the same years from other angles will still need Mao’s Selected Works, Chiang’s diary extracts, and the PRC textbooks already on this shelf; this volume is the party-office synthesis of the 1937–49 arc.

Printed pagination in this edition runs pp. 461–845 for the lower book (the upper book of Vol. I ends before that block). The physical PDF in the wiki root is a full DuXiu-style scan of the lower book only (398 PDF pages including front matter, TOC, body, afterword, and map plates).

Chapter grain is standard official-history: each 章 opens with a period thesis, then numbered 节 (sections) and titled subheads (strategy, base areas, united front, land, campaigns, congresses).

Chapter breakdown (lower book)

Style: English title + (pinyin + 中文) + multi-line gloss of what the chapter actually holds. Section titles follow the book’s own TOC (OCR-corrected where clear).

第四编 — The party in the nationwide War of Resistance (党在全民族抗日战争时期, July 1937 – August 1945)

第十四章 — Realization of the nationwide War of Resistance; the party’s military-strategic course (实现全面抗战和党的军事战略方针)

Printed open p. 461. Frames the jump from partial to full national resistance after July 1937: the strategic choice facing the party, the building of the anti-Japanese national united front with the Guomindang, and the military line for regular and guerrilla war. Early sections walk the outbreak of comprehensive resistance and the establishment of the anti-Japanese national united front; the middle of the chapter treats the party’s military-strategic guidelines and the creation and expansion of behind-enemy-lines base areas; later sections cover united-front work in the Guomindang-held zones, mass mobilization, and the ideological–political work that the office presents as binding the war effort to party leadership of the people’s war.

第十五章 — Upholding the line of resistance, unity, and progress (坚持抗战、团结、进步的方针)

Printed open p. 525. Opens on the arrival of the strategic stalemate after the fall of Guangzhou and Wuhan (October 1938): Japanese force dispersal, economic strain, and the enemy’s shift of political emphasis. Develops the triad 抗战·团结·进步 against 投降·分裂·倒退 — the office’s formula for holding the united front while opposing friction and capitulation. Substantive threads include Japanese strategy adjustment, the long stalemate battlefield, political struggle inside the united front, and the party’s insistence on independent guerrilla warfare and base-area consolidation rather than merger into a single Nationalist command.

第十六章 — Consolidating the anti-Japanese democratic positions (巩固抗日民主阵地)

Printed open ~p. 578 (TOC). Recasts the war after 1941: the German–Soviet war and the Pacific War redraw the international map; China formally declares war on Japan, Germany, and Italy; the global anti-fascist war reaches full scale. Domestically the chapter is about holding and hardening the anti-Japanese democratic base areas under tighter Japanese pressure and Nationalist friction — military defense, regime building, rent-and-interest reduction and related rural policy, party rectification currents, and the linkage between liberated-area construction and the wider Allied war.

第十七章 — Enlarging the liberated areas and striving for final victory in the people’s war of resistance (扩大解放区，夺取人民抗战的最后胜利)

Printed open p. 625. From 1943–44 global turning points (Stalingrad, collapse of the Axis partnership in Europe, Midway and Guadalcanal in the Pacific, Burma counter-offensive) to local counter-offensives on the liberated-area fronts. Covers the growth of liberated-area military and political strength, coordination with the final phase of the war of resistance, and the office’s account of the party positioning itself for the postwar contest as Japan collapses. Terminal matter of the part leads into August 1945 and the strategic map set for the next 编.

第五编 — The party in the nationwide War of Liberation (党在全国解放战争时期, August 1945 – September 1949)

第十八章 — Striving for peace and democracy at home (争取实现国内和平民主)

Printed open p. 673. Post-surrender China as a contest of two destinies: the party’s program for an independent, free, democratic, unified, and prosperous new country versus continued Nationalist one-party rule under U.S. support. International and domestic postwar balances; Chongqing negotiations and related political struggle; the Political Consultative Conference (parties and numbers as the book lists them); the twin issues of political democratization and nationalization of the armed forces; and the breakdown path from truce politics toward full-scale civil war. The chapter is the office’s bridge from anti-Japanese united front to liberation-war polarization.

第十九章 — Smash the Nationalist offensive; put the liberated areas on a war footing (以自卫战争粉碎国民党的军事进攻和加强解放区建设)

Defensive–mobile war in the first phase of the civil war: defeating Nationalist offensives, shifting force ratios, and building the liberated areas for sustained war. Land policy and support for the front; organization of rear and militia; coordination of several strategic theaters. The office’s emphasis is dual — military self-defense victories and internal consolidation so the liberated areas can carry a long war.

第二十章 — The second turning point in Chinese revolutionary history (中国革命历史的第二个转折点)

The strategic turn: from defense to strategic attack, the language of a new high tide of Chinese revolution (as in the inner-party directive 迎接中国革命的新高潮), and the joining of liberated-area warfare with Guomindang-area popular movements as two fronts. Includes land-reform high tide and correction of left excesses (reference back to land-struggle documents; Ren Bishi’s speech on land-reform problems; reports from Xi Zhongxun, Deng Xiaoping, and others as the book cites them), party rectification amid rapid membership growth (toward ~2.7 million members by 1947 in the book’s figures), and the operational narrative that sets up the decisive campaigns — including texture such as the Liaoshen fighting (Jinzhou, Tashan blocking, Changchun’s peaceful liberation) sampled in the body OCR.

第二十一章 — Strategic decisive battles and the founding of New China (战略决战的胜利和中华人民共和国的成立)

The terminal military-political arc: strategic decisive engagements, collapse of major Nationalist formations, advance across the country, and preparations to found the new state. Late sections reach the Second Plenum of the Seventh Central Committee at Xibaipo (March 1949) — attendance scale and the shift from rural encirclement to governing cities — and carry the narrative to September 1949 and the threshold of the PRC. Closing military paragraphs in the sampled text note residual campaigns into 1950 as the large-scale liberation war winds down, while the political climax of the chapter remains the founding moment.

结束语 — Basic experience and great significance of the victory of China’s new-democratic revolution (中国新民主主义革命胜利的基本经验和伟大意义)

A formal conclusion, not a narrative chapter. Distills the office’s canonical lessons: party leadership; the united front; armed struggle; and party building as a “great project” under conditions where most members came from peasants and other non-worker strata and the party long lived in rural base areas — thought, organization, and work-style construction; criticism and self-criticism; rectification; resistance to subjectivism, sectarianism, and left/right political errors. Frames the 1937–49 victory as the vindication of new democracy and the premise of the socialist period (taken up in later official volumes, not in this file).

后记 — Afterword (后记)

Short compilation afterword from the Central Party History Research Office on the making of the volume (sources, collective authorship, edition line). Treat as paratext for provenance, not as narrative history.

Maps (附图)

Terminal situation and campaign maps (OCR captions include, among others, Eighth Route / New Fourth Army and South China guerrilla strategic counter-offensive, August–September 1945; national military situation at victory in the war of resistance, September 1945; route of the shift of the Center in northern Shaanxi, March 1947 – March 1948). Useful as the book’s own spatial digest of the lower-book years.

How this sits on the shelf

  • Upstream in Vol. I (not in this PDF): founding of the party, first civil war / land-revolution period, and the run-up through the Xi’an Incident to mid-1937 live in the upper book of the same first volume.
  • Parallel primary texture: [[mao-zedong-xuanji-4vols|Mao’s Selected Works]] for theoretical and directive voice; Chiang diary selections for the Nationalist high command’s day-grain; [[deng-xiaoping-lushi-li-xinzhi|Deng shilu]] for one marshal-politician’s biographical cut through Taihang and the Central Plains.
  • Downstream official arc: He Qin PRC history and 2022 马工程 PRC history pick up from 1949; this lower book stops at the founding threshold.

Bits of Old China — William C. Hunter

What this is

The 1885 companion to The ‘Fan Kwae’ at Canton before Treaty Days. Hunter’s preface treats the Fankwae as an extinct species: extinguished by treaty, gone from the City of Rams (Canton) as thoroughly as the deinotherium. Chinese notices of the race, he says, run from fiery hair and “red-headed demons” to men from regions of mist; the habitat is dated 1640 to the Treaty of Nanking (1842).

Unlike the 1882 memoir’s single narrative, this volume is a contents-listed sketch-book: factory jokes, shop cards, temples, Macao residents, war incidents, origin-stories of the Middle Kingdom, printing, script, a long Singapore–Malacca travel piece (1825–26), painter George Chinnery, and the Canton Regatta Club. A footnote on the contents page records Ke-ying’s suicide by silken cord as a privilege of imperial-family connection.

Sketch breakdown

Glosses follow the printed order, grounded in this OCR.

Opening cluster (factories and holidays). A Conundrum sets the Thirteen Factories as the sole Fankwae residence: edicts to tremble and not sell “foreign mud,” three monthly walk-days with a linguist (ignored), the fiction that everyone leaves after the tea season. Liberty Day is the sailors’ spree — brandy, Lob-Lob Creek, hoisting Jack aboard drunk. Dinner at Hwa-te Gardens is a New Year feast in verse (bird’s-nest soup, pomfret, Nanking mutton, Honam capon) with a Patrick/Mahoney joke naming Hunter. Factory Square looks out on salt-junk fleets, the salt monopoly (Teen-pak, salt chop-house, provincial cruisers), Junk River and Whampoa.

Law, medicine, dinners, paper. Trial of a Lascar at the Consoo House. Doctor Cox (with Bradford of Philadelphia) called by Pwan-kei-qua to Honam for a sick son; a silver-pheasant card. Trying a Factory — taking a hong on hire. Fankwae dinner, by a Chinese guest: young Lo’s friend writes to Peking of the Suy-Hong table. Shop cards (confectioner; Hukitjee Jimmybhoy scent-beads and vermilion “outside the street of Enormous Serenity”; ink). Letter from an Indian firm. Howqua’s unique joke and jade stone on the same opening.

Merchants, objects, religion. Ming-qua’s military career in comic verse (charter, Peking Gazettes as “security”). Bronze vases and tripods. Capture of a Roman Catholic courier (French mission network). Visit of a mandarin who praises broadcloth and is sent to Old Clothes Street. Black Dragon tea. Temple of Longevity and a later note on its destruction. Tomb of a Spanish priest, Honam — Latin epitaph (MDCLXIX, Friar Gregorios, restored stone). Carpenters’ Square. Thomas Beale. Pwan-kei-qua’s country house (and a 1860 French gazetteer piece on a Canton estate of “Portingus” / opium fortune, which a Chinese interlocutor dismisses as “belong foolo pigeon”).

Rebels, ballads, “institutions.” Rebels at Tsang-poo with two cantos of the Fokeiade (and a “fragment of a monastic ballad”). Hunter and companions climb the Carmen (Garibaldi, guano from Callao) to watch firing on a coast junk. Mender of broken China (Wang Neang). Then short comparative notes as the Chinese explain themselves: public debt, juries, public schools, religion, democracy, Fankwae and Chinese customs.

Origins, Ke-ying, street paper. Origin of the ‘Middle Kingdom.’ Imperial Commissioner Ke-ying (耆英). Perambulating advertisement; reward card (“in search of Le Apeaou”); buffalo’s complaint; Dragon Boat Festival; aborigines; creation of Old China.

Macao and Canton civic scenes. Macao — old residents (long). Drought at Canton. Beheading and cutting in pieces. Quan-shing on the loaves and fishes. Shining midday tea. Escaped convicts from Sydney. Sea Screen Temple. Gold-smelter’s card. ‘Court Journal.’ Le Père Huc: the Abbé, passing as Chinese, then in French; talk of Lhasa, Ke-shen (琦善) as envoy to the Grand Lama, successor to Lin, arriving Canton 29 September 1840, recalled after talks with Captain Elliot. Nankin Jack.

War and the city. Massacre of the crew of the Navigateur: Consoo trial, forty-nine convicted, captain Woo-Kwan “cut to pieces slowly.” Capture of Captain Gribble. Incidents of the war of 1842 (Chinese pictures of steamers with wheels everywhere; ransom verses that the “rebellious demons” were beaten). Foundation of Canton (City of Rams / Five Genii; wall under Song Yingzong, 1067; Hunter’s 1852 visit with Mrs. Hunter, R. W. Read, Orne of Russell & Co., John Darby Gibb). Nine-storey pagodas. Crape manufacturer’s card. Mohammedan mosque. Disaster to the Troughton (fishing-boats as pirates; government recovery of “two tens of thousands of large rounds”). Building a dwelling-house. Destructive fire. Anecdote of Confucius (Tsang-tsze and the round vessel).

Printing, hongs, plan, script. Origin of printing (movable copper types 1722; Shunzhi queue decree and the Tartar garrison “city within the city” at Canton). Hong and security merchants. Ground plan of the Thirteen Factories (REFERENCE plate: Thirteen Factory Street). Chinese New Year. Origin of the Chinese written language (Tsang-Heě, tortoise, seal character of Chou). Jeems Canning and Corkscrew Bacchus.

Long closers. Singapore and Malacca, 1825 and 1826 — the college that was not ready at Singapore; Anglo-Chinese College routine (Scripture in Chinese, library, Mr. Humphreys’s dispensary); elephant kraal; Hindoo hook-swinging. George Chinnery at Macao: nicknames on the Square (William Jardine “Iron-headed Rat,” Latimer “the Gong,” Olyphant “Idol Devil” / “Devil who worshipped God,” Wilcox “high Devil,” Capt. Gover of Jardine’s Samarang “fat Devil,” missionaries Bridgman and Abeel “Old Story-telling Devils”). Canton Regatta Club: Atalanta and Gipsy matches; Macao Roads gale; cutter Fairy down, Capt. Haddock of Glenlyon and two Lascars drowned; Hunter sailing the Gipsy.

Notes for later extraction

Pair with the 1882 memoir for the same names (Howqua, Pwan-kei-qua, Consoo, Thirteen Factories, Lin / Ke-shen / Ke-ying, Russell & Co., Jardine). Unique here: shop-card language, Honam tomb, Huc, Navigateur, Troughton, printing/queue, Singapore–Malacca college year, Chinnery’s nickname list, regatta. Await Proceed before entity/event pages.

A Biography of Sun Yat-sen (Sun Yixian zhuanji 孫逸仙傳記) — trans. Xu Zhiren 徐植仁 (after the original Western biography)

What this is

孫逸仙傳記 (Sun Yixian zhuanji, “Biography of Sun Yat-sen”) is a translated biography of Sun Yat-sen (孫文 / 孫中山; here 孫逸仙) issued as a volume of the 中國國民黨叢書 (Collection of the Chinese Nationalist Party / Kuomintang series). The translator is 徐植仁 (Xu Zhiren), and the volume was published by the 中國文化服務社 (China Cultural Service Agency; 發行人 劉百閔) with an editorial preface to the party series. This is a translation of an original Western-authored biography of Sun — the narrative voice is that of a foreign observer describing China and Sun from the outside (Guangdong as a land of lively Cantonese, Sun as “the child who created the Republic of China”), and the chapter set traces Sun from his 翠亨 village childhood through the fall of the Qing to his 1925 death.

The 1946 imprint records 中華民國三十二年十月 再版 (second printing, October 1943) and 中華民國三十五年一月 再一版 (reissue, January 1946). The cover sheet carries the series title and 孫逸仙傳記, with 譯者 徐植仁. The preface (敘言) is signed 葉楚傖, dated 三二九 (29 March).

Front matter

Series preface (敘言). The Kuomintang series editorial (signed 葉楚傖) explains the purpose of the 中國國民黨叢書: to preserve the party’s revolutionary history and its leaders’ doctrine — from 興中會, 同盟會, 國民黨, 中華革命黨 to 中國國民黨 — and to rally national confidence in 三民主義 under the 總理 and 總裁 (Chiang Kai-shek), amid the simultaneous war of resistance and national construction. It states the series gathers party-historical documents, the leaders’ revolutionary theory and pronouncements, and foreign scholars’ writings that spread the party’s doctrine.

第一期 — Early life in China (早年中國生活, 1866–1889)

Sections 一–十八 describe Sun’s childhood and boyhood.

一 翠亨 (Cuìhēng). Opens with Sun’s native village 翠亨 under a hill in Guangdong — birthplace (the narrative dates his birth 1866, 民國紀元前四十六年, 清同治丙寅十月初六) of “the child who created the Republic of China.” The village’s free, independent spirit and its nearness to Canton and Macao formed Sun’s temperament; an aunt’s tales of foreign ships and a returned overseas merchant’s stories of a golden land beyond the sea stirred his early resolve to study the foreigners.

二 廣東第一 (Guangdong first). A portrait of Guangdong as the southernmost of the eighteen provinces — its wealth, its lively, intelligent, enterprising Cantonese, its overseas communities, and the temperament Sun drew from the region. The biography repeatedly credits Guangdong’s people and émigrés with supporting his revolutionary work.

三–五 正直的父親 / 賢莊的母親 / 命名和住址 (The upright father; the worthy mother; naming and address). Family portraits: Sun’s father as a sober village man, his mother as the anchor of the household, and the naming and circumstances of Sun’s early home.

六–八 幼年的遊戲 / 中山和三字經 / 水災的故事 (Childhood games; Sun and the Three Character Classic; the story of the flood). Sun’s early schooling (including the 三字經 primer), his games, and formative village stories — including a tale of how one hoards treasure against flood and robbery, which Sun took as a practical lesson about the world of taking and keeping.

九–十一 官吏和三個有錯的弟兄 / 白契 / 幼年的搜求真理者 (The official and three erring brothers; the blank deed; the young seeker after truth). Village encounters with officialdom, corruption, and injustice that turned the young Sun’s mind toward truth-seeking and reform.

十二–十四 中山對於改造的第一次努力 / 徑奴中的人們 / 中國就是天下 (Sun’s first effort at reform; the people in bondage; China is the world). Sun’s first attempts at social change — including his boyhood protest against foot-binding for his sister, which the book marks as his first reform act — and observations on village servitude and China’s old conviction that China is “the world.”

十五–十八 大可 / 大哥從火奴魯魯回來 / 饋贈的教訓 / 從海港所得的教訓 (Dake; the elder brother returns from Honolulu; the lesson of gifts; the lesson from the harbor). Family and village episodes, including the return of Sun’s elder brother from Honolulu (a link to his later Hawaiian journey) and lessons drawn from gifts and the harbor at 金星港.

第二期 — Sojourn in Honolulu (在火奴魯魯旅居生活, 1879–1883)

Sections 十九–二十: 火奴魯魯 (Honolulu) and 在夏威夷商務和學校中的時代 (the age of business and school in Hawaii). Sun joins his elder brother, works and studies in the Hawaiian commercial and school setting, and is exposed to Western education and Christianity.

第三期 — Sojourn in Hong Kong (在香港的旅居, 1884–1886)

Sections 二十一–二十五: 皇家的營糧農; 在翅味鄉村中的宣傳工作 (propaganda work in the village); 中山與耶穌教徒的神像 (Sun and the Christians’ images); 皇仁學校裡的英國勢力、中法之戰的一天 (British influence at the Royal School / Queens College; the day of the Sino-French War); 不愛錢 (not loving money). Sun’s Hong Kong schooling, his engagement with Christianity, and the impact of the Sino-French War (中法戰爭) on his thinking.

第四期 — Sun’s profession (中山的職業, 1886–1894)

Section 二十六 職業的選擇、醫學校、宣告獨立、革命死了 (Choosing a profession; the medical school; declaring independence; the revolution dies). Sun’s choice of medicine, his medical studies, and the transition from doctor to revolutionary — the moment his medical career gives way to politics.

第五期 — Early revolutionary life (初期革命生活, 1895–1896)

Section 二十七 第一次懸賞購中山頭、倫敦使館遇難 (The first reward offered for Sun’s head; the London Legation incident). The first bounty on Sun’s head and the celebrated London kidnapping (倫敦被難) at the Chinese legation in 1896.

第六期 — Urgent revolutionary methods (迫切革命方法, 1896–1911)

Sections 二十八–三十二: 山下區一百二十哩; 中山與勞工 (Sun and labor); 演說家的中山、鎮靜的中山、寬想的中山 (Sun as orator, calm, broad-minded); 中山與黃馬; 牢記你到芝加哥去的時光 (remember the time you went to Chicago). Sun’s world tours, his organizing among overseas Chinese and labor, and the propaganda and fundraising that built the pre-1911 revolutionary movement.

第七期 — The provisional presidency (臨時總統任期, 1912–1913)

Sections 三十三–三十四: 中華民國的第一個大總統 (the first provisional president of the Republic of China) and 袁世凱的陰謀 (Yuan Shikai’s intrigues). Sun’s inauguration and the maneuvering by which Yuan Shikai succeeded him.

第八期 — Revolution after the provisional presidency (臨時總統後之革命, 1913–1916)

Section 三十五 袁世凱的末路 (Yuan Shikai’s end). The fight against Yuan — the second revolution (二次革命) and the campaign against the monarchist restoration.

第九期 — Life in Shanghai (在上海的生活, 1916–1918)

Sections 三十六–三十九: 中山對於美國的機會 (Sun’s opportunities toward America); 中山法中的瘋人; 中山的家庭生活（在上海莫利愛路的家） (Sun’s family life at his Moulieux Road home in Shanghai); 結論、中山的主義 (Conclusion: Sun’s doctrine). Sun’s Shanghai years, his writings, and the concluding statement of his 主義.

第十期 — The late revolutionary period (晚年革命時期, 1921–1925)

Sections 四十–四十七: 組織新政府被舉為總統 (organizing a new government and being elected president); 廣州避難 (taking refuge in Guangzhou); 拒絕北上 (refusing to go north); 三民主義宣言 (the Three Principles proclamation); 為什麼離廣州 (why he left Guangzhou); 處置革命之商園; 聯俄 (alignment with Soviet Russia); 逝世 (his death, 1925). The final decade: the Canton governments, the Three Principles, the alliance with Soviet Russia, and Sun’s death.

Source notes

  • Original author: This is a translation of a Western (likely American) biography of Sun Yat-sen — the structure and narrative voice (a foreign author describing Guangdong and “the child who created the Republic of China”) match the well-known translated biography 孫逸仙傳記 rendered by 徐植仁, i.e. the Chinese edition of the 1925 biography Sun Yat-sen and the Chinese Republic. OCR of the scan did not yield an unambiguous printed author line on the front matter, so this identification rests on the structure, the translator’s name, and the known bibliography; treat the attribution as high-confidence by bibliography but not visually re-verified page-by-page in this extract.
